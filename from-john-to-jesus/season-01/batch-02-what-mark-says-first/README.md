## Central evidence question

What does the earliest extant complete Gospel actually say before Matthew, Luke, and John reshape the Jordan story?

## One-sentence claim

Mark's opening moves rapidly from scripture and John in the wilderness to Jesus being baptized by John, the heavens being torn open, the Spirit descending, and a direct heavenly address; later Gospels retain the core while adding, omitting, softening, or reframing specific elements.

## Source notes to preserve

- Mark 1:1 has a genuine textual problem around `υἱοῦ θεοῦ` (“Son of God”); the SBLGNT prints the shorter wording. Ancient witnesses preserve both forms, and priority is debated.
- Mark 1:2 SBLGNT reads “in Isaiah the prophet.” A later majority reading has “in the prophets”; the harder “Isaiah” wording has early and geographically broad support.
- Mark 1:9 directly names John as the one who baptizes Jesus.
- Matthew later adds John's objection and Jesus' reply; Luke narrates the baptism passively and does not name the baptizer in the scene; John reframes the episode as John's remembered witness rather than narrating the immersion itself.
